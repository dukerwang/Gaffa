## Product Overview
**One-liner:** **[Duke]** Direction, not final: the most realistic fantasy football game. Scores match what you watched, transfers follow the real Premier League market, and every season has several trophies.
**What it does:** Gaffa is a dynasty-style fantasy football (soccer) game for private leagues of friends. Players are scored against the baseline for their specific role instead of a flat points table, you buy and sell them at open auctions with a permanent Club Balance, and a league runs a table plus three parallel knockout cups.
**Product category:** Fantasy football (soccer), dynasty format. Buyers search for "dynasty fantasy football soccer," "FPL alternative," "FPL with friends."
**Product type:** Web app (consumer, multiplayer, league-based). Live at gaffa.live.
**Business model:** Free. No monetization intent; Duke's stance is users first, money second. No ads, no entry fees, and past decisions rule out contracts, wages or recurring costs for managers.

## Target Audience
**Target users:** Football fans, whether or not they play FPL. First: FPL players (Gaffa is better) and people who watch football but skip fantasy. Second door: people who play fantasy football or basketball, don't follow soccer, and want the same experience with it.
**Decision-makers:** The commissioner. One person creates the league and recruits 5 to 11 friends, so the commissioner is the real customer.
**Primary use case:** A league with friends where scores match what you watched and knowing footballers (not farming a points quirk) wins. You draft and own your players, as in other fantasy sports.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Own my squad: draft real players that nobody else in my league has, like in fantasy American football or basketball.
- Prove I know football better than my friends.
- Make the real-world transfer window matter to my fantasy team.
- Have something to play for in March, even when I'm out of the title race.
**Use cases:**
- Redraft league: the easy way in. A draft every season, with the transaction systems switched off.
- Dynasty league: the deeper game. One draft ever, then auctions, trades and loans.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** The biggest soccer fantasy game scores the sport like a spreadsheet, so scores often disagree with what you watched. It also lets everyone own the same player and ranks you against millions of strangers instead of your friends.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- FPL: everyone can own the same player (Duke: "lame"), global rank over a friend league, flat points table, three or four generic position buckets, no real market.
- Sleeper dynasty: true dynasty, but sport-generic slots and context-free scoring.
- Card games (Sorare and similar): ownership is a collectible, not a squad you manage against friends. **[Duke]** confirm how you see these.
**What it costs them:** A quiet 90 minutes from a centre-back scores like a clean-sheet masterclass; a brilliant wing-back is scored as a defender. The game feels arbitrary.
**Emotional tension:** "I watched him play well and he scored 2." Being wrong in front of friends for reasons that aren't about football.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Fantasy Premier League (about 13 million managers in 2025/26 per third-party sources, not an official figure). Falls short on depth, realism and permanence.
**Direct (dynasty):** Sleeper and other US dynasty platforms. Strong on dynasty, weak on football-specific scoring and positions.
**Direct (other football games):** Fantrax, Kickbase, Comunio, Biwenger, Sorare. **[Duke]** Research each before writing comparison pages; web search this session returned nothing reliable on them.
**Secondary:** FPL companion tools (AI tip apps). They improve FPL; they don't replace it.
**Indirect:** A WhatsApp group and a pub argument. Football Manager (club-running fantasy without friends).

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
Duke named three as what distinguishes Gaffa (2026-11-07):
- Scores that match what you watched: 12 tactical positions, each judged against its own baseline.
- A transfer market tied to real Premier League arrivals and departures.
- Multiple trophies: three parallel cups beside the league, so every club has something to play for.

Familiar, not distinguishing: you draft and own your players, as in fantasy American football and basketball. FPL is the exception. Say it as reassurance or contrast, never as the headline.

Also true:
- True dynasty: one draft, ever.
- Depth on demand, for example the graph view on the players page. Simple by default.
- Open auction economy where part of every fee recirculates to the league.
**How we do it differently:** Sigmoid rating against position-specific medians, so a player is judged against what his role is expected to produce.
**Why that's better:** Scores that match what you watched; a market that behaves like football's.
**Why customers choose us:** **[Duke]** Unknown. Interviews and polls got little response; learn from behavior in the database and a one-line ask to the most engaged managers.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "I already play FPL." | **[Duke]** Draft in your voice. Direction: you should still try Gaffa because it's better; and in FPL everyone can own the same player, while in Gaffa your roster is yours. Don't call it a "second game." |
| "Too complicated." | Start with redraft: a draft, a lineup, a league. **[Duke]** Draft the full answer once the first-week flow is measured. |
| "I can't get enough friends." | Redraft mode and Caretaker clubs now; open leagues with strangers are planned after the product is refined and proven at scale. |

**Anti-persona:** Players who want a single global rank against millions, and anyone looking for a gambling or paid-entry product.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Everyone owning the same players; scores that don't match the match; no real market.
**Pull:** "Realistic" scoring, a transfer market that follows real news, trophies, and a league with friends where your roster is yours.
**Habit:** FPL is free, familiar and where everyone is; the group chat is already there.
**Anxiety:** Learning a new ruleset; friends won't join; it's new and unproven (8 unique people across two leagues as of 2026-11-07).

## Brand Voice
Draft from Duke's recorded complaints and rules (`docs/DECISIONS.md`), not from copy that has been tested. Inference, not a decision, until Duke confirms. Duke wants all text to read as masterfully written, never as "Claude speak."

| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Plain | Names the thing: "Title-Winning XI," "Transactions." | Blunt, or short for its own sake | "Top Rated" |
| Football-literate | Real players, matches and terms: clean sheet, wing-back, deadline day | Jargon the casual fan has to decode; depth sits one click down | "Out of 357 rated players" |
| Specific | A real example with the real figure beats a claim | Superlatives, "first," stacked promises | Ødegaard 8.90, 1 goal, 25.6 pts |
| Confident, quiet | States what Gaffa does | Hype words, urgency, exclamation marks | "Fantasy football, played like the real thing." |
| Simple first | The casual fan understands it on one read | Dumbing down; the data stays available | Redraft as the easy door |

**Writing tests (from Duke's complaints):** no UI element as the subject of a verb ("Enter opens it"); no "lets you" or "allows you to"; Title Case for names and buttons; headings name the thing instead of narrating it; no "it's not X, it's Y" reveals, "no X, no Y, no Z" lists, or sentences that trail into extra clauses; no eyebrow lines; cut anything a real person wouldn't say out loud.
